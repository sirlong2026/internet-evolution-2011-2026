### Figure 1 | Four-layer survival cascade from the 2011 snapshot to the 2026 live network

**(a)** Address lineage of the stratified cohort. Of 199 candidate prefixes drawn from the 2011 snapshot, 178 (89.4%) remain covered in the 2026 routing view, 90 of them survive as identical prefixes; the measurement set comprises 100 addresses (57 exact survivors and 43 covered-only). 35 of the 100 answer ICMP echo requests (25 of the 57 exact survivors, 10 of the 43 covered-only). 1 address completes a TLS handshake, and its certificate belongs to an unrelated third party, so 0 of the 100 still serve the 2011 organization's TLS identity. Bars show absolute counts; the red marker marks the empty terminal stage.
**(b)** Identity bridge. Of the 100 measured addresses, 51 carry 2011 hostname mappings, yielding 48 registrable domains; 34 (70.8%) still resolve in 2026 and 15 (44.1%) complete a TLS handshake, of which 9 are organization-identity leaves that entered the 48-hour recurring campaign.
**(c)** Bridge survivors by edge class: direct 12/24; major CDN 0/6; Google Cloud 1/2; AWS 2/2. CDN-fronted domains reject the SNI-less handshake, which is why the campaign is restricted to direct and cloud edges. Filled bars count completed handshakes; hatched bars count domains that refused or failed to handshake.
**(d)** Path layer. Of 500 executed probe paths, 180 reach the target address; every path to an ICMP-live destination reaches its destination (180/180) and no path to an ICMP-dead destination does (0/320), so reachability carries no information beyond address liveness. 18 of 50 traced destinations were reached, all of them live.

*Source data:* published derived statistics of the artifact package (cohort survival, address-layer summary, path-layer summary, DNS-bridge feasibility, TLS scout summary).

### Figure 2 | Prefix-length survival of the 2011 snapshot in the 2026 routing view

**(a)** Survival of the 713,853 prefixes of the 2011 snapshot in the 1,164,330-unique-prefix 2026 routing view (×1.631 growth) as a function of the 2011 prefix length. Aggregate survival (covered by any 2026 prefix) falls monotonically with length, from 87.1% for /16 and shorter to 66.3% at /24, but exact survival (the identical prefix still announced) does not: it is 20.6% for /21–/23 and rises to 31.1% at /24, because deaggregation makes /24 the stable unit of end-site announcement. The collapse beyond /24 is a caliber artifact rather than decay, because the 2011 vantage retained more-specifics that the 2026 DFZ collector filters. Prefix lengths with fewer than 500 snapshot prefixes are omitted.
**(b)** Aggregate survival in the 2026 routing view against the same-day control, the public DFZ view collected on the snapshot's own date (377,201 prefixes, 45 peers). The > /24 bucket is 22.8% covered in the 2026 view against 99.3% in the same-day control, so the collapse is a collector filter-policy artifact rather than decay.
**(c)** Exact survival against the same-day control. The /24 excess is present in both epochs (31.1% against 20.6% for /21–/23 in the 2026 view; 50.8% against 45.0% in the control), which places the effect in announcement granularity rather than in fifteen years of change.

*Source data:* published survival and same-day calibration statistics of the artifact package. Percentages are rounded to one decimal; the bucket denominators are given in Table 2 of the manuscript.

### Figure 3 | Per-target certificate campaign and fingerprint-set verdicts

**(a)** Per-target outcome of the 48-hour recurring certificate campaign. Bars count successful TLS handshakes over the merged observation set of each target (the union of all its measurement IDs); labels give the number of hourly rounds and the number of certificate-carrying vantages. Eight targets show a fleet-wide union of exactly one leaf certificate (filled bars); target A shows two leaf fingerprints with identical subject and validity interval but different issuers (hatched bar), which the variant-attribution rule classifies as vantage-side re-signing. Target A's measurement was force-stopped after 6 hourly rounds on credit exhaustion and resumed under a second measurement ID, leaving a gap in its observation window.
**(b)** Fingerprint set and engine verdict. A single leaf fingerprint is the structural terminal state (indistinguishable). Two fingerprints permit a verdict only when the observations separate the hypotheses: the cloud anchor, whose two pre-registered deployment certificates are known, yields decidable with the round-robin hypothesis at κ = 1/3 exactly, replicated on the laboratory deployment; target A yields conditionally decidable with the source-sticky two-backend hypothesis. The anchor measurement shows a union of 2 fingerprints with 0 stray observations across 53 hourly slots.

*Source data:* published per-target judgment, window statistics, target labels and anchor verdict of the artifact package. Targets are reported by anonymized label A–I; the label-to-target mapping is released with the package.
